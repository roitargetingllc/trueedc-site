How to Build an EDC Kit, Step by Step

A simple, budget-first way to build an everyday carry kit: pick a cutting tool, a light, something to write with and a way to organize your keys.

The short answerBuild an EDC kit by starting from what you already carry, then adding one item for each problem you hit often: a cutting tool, a light, a pen and a key organizer. Keep the whole kit light enough that you never leave it at home.

Step 1: Pick your cutting tool

Decide between a small multitool and a folding knife. A multitool is the safer bet for offices, travel days and anywhere knives draw looks. A folding knife cuts better and opens faster.

If you're not sure, start with a keychain-size tool like the Victorinox Classic SD or Leatherman Micra.

Step 5: Carry it for a week, then adjust

After a week, drop anything you never touched and upgrade the thing you used most. That is how a kit stays small and useful.

Questions people ask

How much does a basic EDC kit cost?

A useful starter kit with a small tool, light, pen, notebook and key clip can come in under $100. See our first EDC kit under $100 for one example.

Should my first EDC tool be a knife or a multitool?

A small multitool is the easier first pick because it is welcome in more places. Choose a folding knife if you cut things often and your local laws allow it.

Where do I carry EDC gear?

Most people use a front pocket for a clipped knife or light, the key ring for small tools, and a back or chest pocket for a pen and notebook.
